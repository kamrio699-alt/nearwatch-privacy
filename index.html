Nearwatch privacy policy

Effective 15 November 2026

Nearwatch is an Android app that shows dangerous events around the world and alerts you when something dangerous happens near an area you choose. This policy explains what the app does with your information.

Contact: nearwatchsupport@gmail.com

The short version

  • Free, with no ads or in-app purchases.
  • No account, no sign-in.
  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking SDKs.
  • Your home area and settings are stored only on your phone. They are excluded from cloud backups and device transfers.
  • Nearwatch never sells or shares your data.

Location

Nearwatch uses a home area to decide what is "near you". You can set it in two ways:

  1. Approximate location, if you allow it. Nearwatch requests only the approximate location permission (ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION, accurate to roughly 1 to 3 km). It reads your location only while the app is open, and saves the result on your phone as your home area.
  2. By hand, by pressing and holding a point on the map.

Nearwatch does not request background location and never reads your location while the app is closed. Background checks use the saved home area.

To find official warnings for your area, Nearwatch sends a rounded position (two decimal places, about 1 km) to these public services, and only these:

  • UK Environment Agency flood monitoring API, if your home area is in the United Kingdom
  • US National Weather Service alerts API, if your home area is in the United States

These requests contain no identifier apart from the technical information every internet request carries, such as your IP address. Each service handles that information under its own privacy policy.

The country name of your home area also decides which official warning feed (MeteoAlarm) and which news feeds the app downloads. Those downloads contain no location.

Information Nearwatch downloads

Nearwatch downloads public information from earthquake, disaster and weather agencies, news outlets' public RSS feeds, public service status pages and OpenFreeMap map tiles. These are ordinary downloads. Nearwatch sends them nothing about you beyond what any internet request carries.

Notifications

There is no Nearwatch server. Your phone checks the public feeds itself and decides whether to show each alert, using your home area and settings, which stay on the device.

Wear OS watch

If you install Nearwatch on a paired Wear OS watch, the phone app sends it a short summary (the status line and recent events, including distances from your home area) over the Bluetooth/Wi-Fi link between your own devices. It isn't sent anywhere else.

Data on your phone

Nearwatch stores on your phone:

  • your home area and its place name
  • your settings
  • a list of events you have already been notified about
  • a cache of recently downloaded events and feeds

You can delete all of it at any time in Settings → Your data → Delete my data, or by uninstalling the app.

Children

Nearwatch is not directed at children under 13.
